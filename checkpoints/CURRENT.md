**The live tracker learns travel times but never measured how far off its predictions are, and there was no place to see either (#11, branch `feat/11-tracker-scoring`, task NMA-0060; owner, 2026-11-14: "do we also have a graph or any dashboard for it").** The observer job that runs on the VPS every 15 minutes (`scripts/transit-observe.ts`) now also scores the tracker. For trains it compares the feed's arrival time and the blended time that moves the drawn train (`blendStops`) with when the train reports standing at the stop. For buses it predicts when a bus leaves later stops the way the map's dead reckoning moves it, for 16 routes a run, and compares that with when the bus is seen leaving. A capped sample goes to `raw.transit_prediction_scores` (400 rows a system a run, 21 days) and three small views are refreshed after each run. Two endpoints serve them: `/api/transit-health/coverage` (stretches with learned times and samples per line or route, schedule and time band) and `/api/transit-health/accuracy` (median error and share within a minute, with learned times and without). A 7-minute dry run on 2026-11-14 scored 353 train predictions (median 25 s off with learned times, 27 s with the feed alone) and 45 bus predictions (39 s off by default, and late more often than early). Seen on the way: a bus line's shape carries the stops on both kerbs, so the map's bus motion stops at stops the bus does not serve, and only 29 of M22's 102 learned stretches join stops that are neighbours on its shape. The page is at `/tracker-health` (branch `feat/11-tracker-health-page`), reached from a Tracker health row in Settings that shows only while Advanced is on, or by its address: coverage per subway line and bus route (stretches with 5 or more samples, stretches with fewer, sample counts), the three schedules by six time bands, one line's stretches in stop order with station names, and the accuracy panel, which says "Not enough data yet" with the count until 200 predictions are scored. Every chart names its endpoint and load time. The page is its own 5.8 kB chunk; the boot chunk's budget went from 294 to 294.5 kB for the Settings row and its label in eight languages. Both parts are live since 2026-11-15: the first run stored 740 scores (trains a median 30 s off with learned times and 32 s with the feed alone; buses 72 s and about a minute late). On the live page the 1 train's stretches showed both directions in one list, because the feed gives that line one direction number both ways; the page now reads a train's direction from its platform id (branch `fix/11-line-direction`). Each score now also stores the conditions at the time (branch `feat/11-score-conditions`; owner, 2026-11-14: "traffic, time of day, weather etc"): whether a notice was in effect for the line, rain or snow from the app's weather answer (one reading for the city per run), and for a bus the DOT speed link within 80 m of the stop. The accuracy answer splits error by them and by rush hour, lists the worst-predicted stops, and `/api/transit-health/hours` gives samples per single hour. Nothing in a prediction uses them. A backtest on 398,216 subway observations from 2026-11-12 to 11-15 found the band pool, the single hour and an adaptive rule equally good (median 8 s off each); only the L gains from the hour, so the estimator is unchanged. The MTA publishes bus travel times between timepoints by route, day and hour (data.ny.gov `kufs-yh3x`, 10.7 million rows from 2025-01; Open NY's terms allow any lawful reuse with no attribution). The job now imports the last three published months for 8 routes a run into `raw.mta_bus_segment_speeds`, under 200,000 rows in all, and `/api/transit-health/bus-speeds` serves a route's averages (branch `feat/11-mta-bus-speeds`). Timepoints are major stops, so one MTA segment spans several of our stretches (the M15 SBS has three segments each way). The page also has a Stations card (branch `feat/11-station-view`; owner, 2026-11-14: "each station is different"): every station least-learned first, with the ones that have no data or a thin stretch marked, and for a chosen station each line and direction with the time in from the previous stop, the dwell there and the time out to the next. Subway station ids of one name within 400 m are grouped as one place (447 places from 496 ids); a bus stop is shown for the chosen route only. Tapping a stretch in One line opens its station. The page shows the rest of it (same branch): under the band grid, a strip of the 24 hours of each schedule with each hour's samples and the share of stretches that have 8 or more in that hour alone; in the accuracy panel, error by rush hour, notice, weather and traffic by the stop, and the ten worst-predicted stops by name; and for a bus route, the MTA's published average between timepoints beside our learned times summed over the stops between.
